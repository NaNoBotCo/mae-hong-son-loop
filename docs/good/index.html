Why you wouldThe good part

Six hundred kilometres of mountain road with something worth stopping for every twenty minutes of it.

Road 1263, above Mae Chaem

5.71curves per kilometre on Route 1095

Four curves a kilometre, for four days

A steering input every few seconds, for four days. That is the whole attraction.

Takeaway · CC BY-SA 3.0

1056 curves over 184.9 kilometres. 110 of them hairpins. On a road this tight engine size stops mattering and line choice starts, which is why a 110cc step-through is as absorbing here as anything with four times the power. You are never not doing something.

The riding is the obvious pleasure and it is not the biggest one. What the circuit actually gives you is a rhythm: you are somewhere different every evening, the days are short enough to stop constantly, and there is something worth stopping for roughly every twenty minutes. Nobody rides this loop fast twice.

Pang Ung, six in the morning

The ten minutes after you stop

Nutchana · CC BY-SA 4.0

The province is quiet in a way that is hard to find. Mae Hong Son has the lowest population density of any province in Thailand — twenty-two people per square kilometre across nearly thirteen thousand of them. On the long southern leg you can ride for half an hour and meet four vehicles.

And the thing nobody puts in the itinerary: the ten minutes after you stop. You get off, the engine ticks as it cools, your hearing comes back, and whatever you climbed for is just sitting there. That happens six or seven times a day on this road. It is the whole thing.

Jong Kham, after dark

A day built round the stops

Stephanie Ecate steffny0206 · CC0 1.0

06:00 The morning market. Curry in a bag, sticky rice, coffee. It is packing up by nine. 08:00 Ride. The first two hours are the clearest air and the emptiest road of the day. 10:00 The stall at the top of the climb. Twenty minutes, a bench, a valley. 12:00 Noodles, wherever you are. Khao soi if it has not gone; nam ngiao if it has. 14:00 Stop riding. The afternoon is for the town you landed in. 16:00 Hot water on the forearms, or a temple with nobody in it, or a hammock. 18:00 Whatever the town does in the evening. In Mae Hong Son that is two lit temples reflected in a pond.

What you eat

ของย่างริมทาง

The roadside grill is the most reliable good meal between towns, and you smell it before you see it.

ขนมจีนน้ำเงี้ยว

Fermented rice vermicelli under a thin, sour, red broth of tomato and pork, with dried kapok flower, pork blood cubes and a pork rib.

ข้าวซอย

A coconut curry noodle soup of upper northern Thailand, Laos and the Shan States. Chicken or beef, pickled mustard greens, shallot, lime, chilli…

ข้าวเหนียว

Glutinous rice, steamed rather than boiled, eaten with the right hand in small pressed balls. The everyday grain of the north and the northeast.

อาหารยูนนานที่บ้านรักไทย

Chinese Yunnanese food, on a reservoir, a few kilometres from the Myanmar border, at the end of a road that goes nowhere else.

ตลาดเช้า

Every town on this loop has one. Rice, curries in bags, grilled things, sticky rice, coffee, and whatever the hills sent down that morning.

Before the coffee, these hills grew tea. Much of it still does, and the older use of the leaf is not a drink at all.

เซเว่น

In the towns, the convenience store is a coffee stop, a water stop, a shade stop and a toilet. It is also the most reliable of the four.

เพิงกาแฟบนยอดดอย

Somewhere near the top of most long climbs on this circuit there is a shack selling coffee, with a bench pointed at the valley.
